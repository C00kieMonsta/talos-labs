# Talos Labs agent design: more it can do, every step visible, one tap per change

DESIGN-WATCH made Rust the agent: a timer, fixed probes, a baseline, a pure rating, and a model that is a one-shot judge naming ids. DESIGN-CHAT made the chat the place to say what you want and turned the model's answers into closed intents the app draws as cards. Both keep the agent invisible: the model's probe choices run after the call and feed the next cycle without a word (`run_model_probes` in `apps/desktop/src-tauri/src/watch/cycle.rs`), a fix shows one status word at a time (`STATUS.*` in `apps/desktop/src/guided/copy.ts`), and nothing the person can ask for starts a round of looking. This document adds the capabilities the owner asked for as closed intents with visible activity: a person-started investigation round, a plan that runs step by step in the chat with a running checklist, verify-after-fix rechecks drawn as rows, a closer watch for an hour, "ask me again tomorrow" and "remind me on Sunday", a write-up for someone else, "what if I leave the disk alone?" answered from the plan's points, and two new kinds of activity row (a probe re-read, a section re-read) on the rows DESIGN-CHAT-UI already draws, fed by the watch and the scan machine, consumed by the chat and the guided step frame.

This document extends `docs/DESIGN-CHAT.md` (the intent set, the cards, the chips), `docs/DESIGN-WATCH.md` (section 3 step 7, the probe round), `docs/DESIGN-WATCH-WINDOW.md` (the closer-watch line), `docs/DESIGN-AI-PRESENCE.md` (moment 5, the order) and `docs/DESIGN-ACCEPT.md`. `docs/SECURITY-MODEL.md` changes in the places listed under "Docs touched by the build" (section 4). Everything else holds: Rust collects and judges; the model only explains and proposes ids from closed sets the app put in the request; it never runs anything and never receives machine text; every Mac change is a Rust catalogue entry run after the person's tap, admin ones through macOS's own dialog; no shell, no root, no file contents beyond the declared exceptions; the app is complete with AI off; one primary per guided screen and one live card in the chat; no notification beyond the watch's act now; nothing leaves the Mac except the person's own AI calls; British English, no em-dashes, the banned-word list in `apps/desktop/src/guided/copy.test.ts`; every model text carries the provider's name and "Not verified"; model text never becomes a button, a headline or a row label. Facts about the tree were checked on 2026-10-17 against the files named; `cycle.rs` and `watch/mod.rs` are modified in the working tree, so line numbers there may move.

The owner's words, verbatim (2026-10-17): "Keep iterating on the talos UI -- the chat is bad, we need a better chat interface that you would have on a real modern agent driven app -- we need more playful interactions -- we need more of the threat detection strategies in place -- adding more agentic capabilities -- add threat detection strategies and interpretation of daily checks". This document is the "more agentic capabilities" piece (TASKS #23). The chat surface itself is DESIGN-CHAT-UI, the threat strategies DESIGN-THREATS-V2, the daily reading DESIGN-DAILY, the motion DESIGN-PLAYFUL; this document names where it meets each and designs nothing twice.

Decisions taken where the brief left room:

- **"Agentic" means Rust does more, and shows it; the model never gains a tool.** Every capability here is a Rust command or a pure app action with a closed argument list (an id, an enum, a minute count). The model's part stays what DESIGN-WATCH gave it: up to three probe ids and the order of up to three intents. A modern agent app shows its work as a list of steps with spinners and ticks; Talos can be honest about that only for things the app itself does, so the activity rows are the app's own events, never a model's narration of them.
- **This document owns the activity timeline; DESIGN-CHAT-UI owns how a row is drawn.** `ActivityEvent`, `ActivityKind`, `reduceActivity`, `rowsFor` and `activityLabel` live in `packages/core/src/activity/` (section 4), fed by `apps/desktop/src/activity/useActivity.ts` from the scan machine's `fix_progress` and `recheck_*` actions, the show card's watcher, `WatchState.checking` and the new `watch:activity` event. DESIGN-CHAT-UI owns the row's DOM (`li.chat-activity-row[data-state][data-activity]` in `ul.chat-activity[role="list"]`), its icons, its state word (`activityWord(event)` through `CHAT.word`) and the labels of the two watch-side kinds (`CHAT.activity.watching`, `CHAT.activity.watchCheck`); the fix-row labels are this document's `ACTIVITY.rows` and `ACTIVITY.verbs`. A row exists only for a phase Rust reports: admin work is one row that keeps the word "your password" until the outcome (DESIGN-CHAT-UI, "Fix what you can"). Rows live in the transcript for one check-up (DESIGN-CHAT decision "The transcript lives for one check-up and never touches disk") and are never written anywhere.
- **"Look into this" costs one judge call, person-started, counted, and works with AI off.** On a watch item it asks Rust for an investigation: one cycle with the model gate forced open (one call under the existing `DAILY_CAP` 24 in `cycle.rs:39`), the model's up to three probe choices run at once as rows, and a code-only re-rate follows with no second call. With AI off, or when the cap is reached, Rust runs the item's own probe set (`probes::investigation_set`, a static table in `apps/desktop/src-tauri/src/watch/probes.rs`, section 4) and the rows are the same. DESIGN-WATCH-WINDOW dropped an app-started `watch_explain` task for competing with the judge's cap; this call is person-started, counted under the same cap, and refused in words at the cap, which is the difference. On a check-up item it is a recheck of the rule's `requires` sections (`core.scan.recheck(sections)`, as `checkAgain` in `apps/desktop/src/guided/runFix.ts` does), zero calls, behind `gate.has("recheck")` as `FindingDetail.tsx:206` gates a recheck today (TASKS #14: the licence buys fixes, rechecks and the watch). The model never decides whether to investigate; the person's tap does.
- **A plan executes one tap per Mac change, never one tap for the plan.** "Do them all" (DESIGN-CHAT chip) walks `plan.steps` one live card at a time; each fix card keeps its own primary and macOS keeps its own dialog; a batch is the one batching that exists (one `osascript`, SECURITY-MODEL Boundary 4). The checklist under the walk is `GuidedList` rows with `DoneRow` ticks (`apps/desktop/src/guided/state.ts`, `GuidedList.tsx`). Nothing chains admin dialogs.
- **Verify-after-fix is already the rule and already a row.** `fix.apply.recheck`, `rulePasses` and `STATUS.checking` exist; the recheck is the `rechecking` row ("Checking it worked", `ACTIVITY.rows.checking`), drawn by DESIGN-CHAT-UI. The step frame draws the same rows; this document adds the "Check again" chip after `still_failing` and nothing else.
- **A closer watch for an hour is the existing closer watch with a minute count.** `close_watch` in `watch/mod.rs:958` sets `close_watch_until` to 24 h (`CLOSE_WATCH_MS`, `cycle.rs:47`) at 15 minutes (`CLOSE_WATCH_MINUTES`, `cycle.rs:48`). `watch_close_watch(minutes: 60 | 1440)` is the one new argument; the interval stays 15. It is offered only when the watch is active (`enabled && licensed`) and `watch.everyMinutes` is not already 15, and refused in words otherwise.
- **Reminders are snoozes with a date and a sentence, never a notification.** "Ask me again tomorrow" and "Remind me on Sunday" write `snoozed[ruleId]` to 09:00 local on that day and `reminders[ruleId]` to the same date in `GuidedMemory` (`packages/core/src/guided/memory.ts`). They surface where the app already speaks: the chat's opening line on the first launch on or after that date, the plan's step order, the end screen's "Still waiting" rows, and the watch window's "Up next" row once DESIGN-WATCH-WINDOW's `next` push exists. The no-nagging rule stands: only the watch's act now may notify.
- **The write-up is authored text, copied; saving is a Rust dialog.** "Write this up" assembles plain text from the catalogue's `guided.headline`, `guided.why` and `guided.done` strings, the score and grade words, the dates, and the watch's state word. `guided.where` is left out: the recipient is not at the Mac, and a settings path is a path. No path, share name, label or port is in it: the 022 folder name is report evidence and is left out on purpose. The model's plan reading (`last-brief.json`, DESIGN-AI-PRESENCE) is included only as a labelled quote with its full badge when it exists. The primary copies to the clipboard through the `CopyButton` pattern (`apps/desktop/src/components/CopyButton.tsx`). The link "Save as a file…" calls a new Rust command that opens macOS's own save panel; the webview never names a path, and Rust refuses the text when it carries a URL, a path or a command line. Open question 1.
- **"What if I leave X?" is answered by the app from points, zero calls, on every provider.** The score is `computeScore` over statuses and points; leaving one rule changes nothing today, so the honest answer is what the rule costs the score and what the catalogue says could happen. A what-if grammar routes the typed question before any call, like DESIGN-CHAT's accept grammar; the model may also name `what_if` as an intent and the app draws the same card.
- **The model's order is honoured within what the app allowed, and nowhere else.** DESIGN-CHAT's "most useful first" over at most three intents stands: the first survivor is the live card. DESIGN-AI-PRESENCE moment 5 keeps the Overview's suggested order behind its button. `plan.steps` order never moves; "Do them all" walks the plan's order, not the model's.
- **A refusal is an app line keyed by reason, with one way out.** `cannot_from_here`, `watch_off`, `full_version`, `full_version_recheck`, `cap_reached`, `limit_reached`, `no_item`, `busy` (section 3). Never model prose.

## 1. Principles

- **Visible, honest, bounded.** Every row in the activity list is something the app did: a probe it ran, a section it re-read, a command it ran after a tap. The row's text is authored, the state is a word and an icon, the time is the app's clock. The model's `probes[].reason` may sit under a probe row as a grey line with the short badge, inside the investigation message only; it never becomes the row, and it never sits inside a card between the authored sentence and the primary (DESIGN-CHAT's placement amendment).
- **One tap per Mac change, as today.** A plan that executes is a sequence of cards, each with its own primary and its own macOS dialog. Nothing runs from a stream, from a chip, or from an intent at validation time (DESIGN-CHAT section 1).
- **Person-started, counted, capped by the provider.** The document adds no app-started call. "Look into this" is one counted judge call; typed turns are the person's own; everything else is zero calls. `limit_reached` and the watch's daily cap are the stops, and both are said in words.
- **Complete with AI off.** Every capability runs on the fixed rules: the investigation runs the item's own probe set, the plan walks, the recheck rows draw, the closer watch ticks, the reminder returns, the write-up assembles. The rows then carry no badge; they are the app's own events, as every app line is.
- **Ids only across every boundary.** A command takes a signal id, a rule id, a minute count or a closed word. The one free-text argument is the write-up's authored text on its way to the save panel; it is authored by the app from catalogue strings and checked in Rust for URLs, paths and command lines before it is written.
- **No nagging.** A reminder is a line on the day, once; a closer watch ends on its own; an investigation never re-runs itself. Nothing in this document notifies.

## 2. The capabilities, state by state

Mock-ups are the chat home of DESIGN-CHAT on the dev machine, licensed, ChatGPT connected, unless noted. Rows marked ○ are running (the static `LoaderCircle`, `ICONS.spinner`, spinning only outside reduced motion), ✓ done (`ICONS.check` in the done bullet), ✗ did not change or could not check (`ICONS.close`), · waiting. Each row is DESIGN-CHAT-UI's `li.chat-activity-row[data-state][data-activity]` (icon slot, label, trailing state word) with that document's icons; the state word is in the text, never in the icon alone. A done row draws the kit tick with DESIGN-PLAYFUL's 240 ms `guided-tick`; static under reduced motion. Rule ids are for this document only.

### 2a. Activity rows under a fix card

The fix card's rows are the events of section 4, drawn as DESIGN-CHAT-UI section 2 ("Fix what you can") draws them: `preparing` ("Getting ready", `ACTIVITY.rows.preparing`) at the tap; for an admin block one `fixing` row with no rule ("Fixing {n} things", `ACTIVITY.rows.batchAdmin`) that keeps the state word "your password" (`CHAT.word.password`) until the outcome, because Rust emits nothing between `WaitingForPassword` and the result; for a single admin fix the verb row (`ACTIVITY.verbs[guided.verb]`, "Turning it on") with the same word; for a user-level item the verb row with "{i} of {n}" (`CHAT.word.running`) per `Running` step; then `rechecking` ("Checking it worked", `ACTIVITY.rows.checking`) for the verify-after-fix recheck. The words by state and `FixError` are CHAT-UI's `activityWord`. This document adds nothing to a single fix or a batch in the chat. It adds one place the same rows render: the guided `StepFrame` in the `working` state, in place of the one status line (`StepFrame.tsx` line 111, `.guided-status`), as `ul.guided-activity` of `li` rows with the same classes. `STATUS.password` stays the step frame's headline sentence while the box is up. The rows' text is announced by one `p.visually-hidden[role="status"].guided-activity-status`, besides the heading announcer at line 105. Rows never re-order. Rows stay in the log under their card; Developer mode adds the timings.

### 2b. "Look into this": the investigation round

Offered as a chip on a watch item (DESIGN-CHAT "The watch's act now" message and the look state), as a chip under a card the app answered with, and as an intent (`investigate`). On a watch item:

> **Look into this**
> I'll re-read the areas this touches and tell you what changed.
> ✓ Background programs · same
> ✓ Open network ports · changed
> ✓ Scheduled jobs · same
> ┃ A new start-up item and a new port in the same hour is the pair worth checking.  ChatGPT, not verified
> Still one thing to look at.
>
> ▍ **A new program started running on its own**
> I noticed this at 14:40. It was not there at the last check. I have not deleted anything. [See what to do]
> This is fine, I know it

The investigation message is one app message: the lead `ACTIVITY.investigate.lead`, the rows, the grey line and the state line. It ends at the state line and never carries a primary or a chip that runs a catalogue entry. When the re-rated state is look or act, the watch card of DESIGN-CHAT section 5 is appended as a separate app message after it, with its own headline and sentence between any model text and the primary. One row per probe Rust ran, in the order it ran them, `ActivityKind` `probe`, labelled by `WATCH_PROBE_LABELS[probeId]` (`apps/desktop/src/app/labels.ts:436`) with the first letter raised; the state word is `same`, `changed` or `couldn't check` (`ACTIVITY.outcome.*`) from the final `cycle::compare` of that cycle (section 4), never a value. The grey line under a row, when the judge gave `probes[].reason` and it passed `safe_sentence` (`validate.rs:57`), is a `p.guided-model-line` with `AI_BADGES_SHORT`, drawn only inside the investigation message; the watch window never draws it. The state line is `ACTIVITY.investigate.calm` "Nothing changed in what I re-read.", `.look(n)` "Still one thing to look at." or "Still {n} things to look at." (n is the look-rated item count, as `WATCH.lookOne` / `lookMany` count), `.act` "This needs doing now.". The model sees the same context as a timer tick (no field names the item); the focus is the app's: when the model's choices do not include the item's own probe, `investigation_set(signal)[0]` is added as a code-chosen row. Budget: one judge call when AI is on and the cap allows (section 5); with AI off, at the cap, in a provider's limit window or with no provider connected, the rows are `investigation_set(signal)` and no grey line appears; `cap_reached` and `limit_reached` are said in words (section 3), the other two say nothing. The tap returns at once (`queued: true`) and the rows arrive as the loop reaches the round; while a cycle is already running the lead reads `ACTIVITY.investigate.wait` "Checking now, one moment." until the first row. A second tap while a round is on screen or a cycle runs scrolls to that round and starts nothing. On a check-up item the same shape with one row per section in `finding.requires`, `ActivityKind` `section`, labelled by `SECTION_PROGRESS_LABELS[key]` and the state word `still off` or `now on` from `rulePasses`, zero calls; without `gate.has("recheck")` the chat answers `refuse.full_version_recheck` and no chip is offered.

### 2c. The plan, step by step, with a running checklist

"Do them all" (DESIGN-CHAT chip) appends one app message holding the checklist, then the first live card under it:

> **Do them all**
> I'll go through these one at a time. Each fix is one tap, and macOS asks for your password when it needs it.
> 0 of 5 done
> ○ The firewall is off
> · Your Mac answers strangers on the network
> · App Store apps do not update on their own
> and 2 more
> ▍ **The firewall is off** … [Turn it on] · Do this later · ▸ Explain this

The checklist is `GuidedList` rows (`GuidedRowIcon` pending, ok, failed) keyed by step id, one per undone step in `plan.steps` order, batch steps expanded to their rules. Visible rows are the live row and the next two, then a trailing "and {n} more" row in `GuidedList`'s `more` style; settled rows fold into the `ACTIVITY.plan.progress` line "2 of 5 done" above the list, so the list and the live card fit above the 600 px fold at 900x600. The live card is the step's card as DESIGN-CHAT draws it; its activity rows (2a) draw inside the card, not in the checklist. When the card resolves, its checklist row ticks (or crosses with the tag "Did not change" or "I'll show you where", as `DoneRow.tag` does today) and the next card appends after `AUTO_ADVANCE_MS`. A show card does not auto-advance: its watcher or "Check again" settles it; "What's next?" moves on and the row keeps ○ with the tag "Waiting for you". "Do this later" on a card marks the row · with the tag "Another day". The person may tap any visible pending row to make it the live card out of order: DESIGN-CHAT section 7's `row_promoted(rowId)` action, with the step id as the row id; the walk continues from the row after the one resolved. The checklist message is updated in place (rows are keyed) so the log is not re-announced; the status element announces the progress line. When every row has settled the end message of DESIGN-CHAT appends under the list. One live card at a time holds throughout; the harness assertion of DESIGN-CHAT section 8 is unchanged.

### 2d. Verify-after-fix

No new mechanism. Every fix card and every batch card ends its activity list with the `rechecking` row (2a). The chat gains one chip after a `still_failing` result: "Check again" (zero calls, `checkAgain`), beside "Show me where" when the rule has a pane; the chip is offered only with `gate.has("recheck")`, and a typed "check again" without it answers `refuse.full_version_recheck`. The step frame shows the same row. In Developer mode the row's grey line names the sections re-read (`SECTION_LABELS`).

### 2e. "Watch closer for an hour"

Chip "Watch closer for an hour" (24 characters) after a watch message in the look state, after the fishy flow's recover screen ran, and after "This is fine, I know it"; also the intent `close_watch` and the typed form (section 4 grammar):

> **Watch this closer for an hour**
> ✓ I'll check every 15 minutes until 15:40.
> [What's next?] [Show me the list]

The headline is `ACTIVITY.closer.headline`; the line is `ACTIVITY.closer.until(time)` with `formatTime` (`labels.ts:240`). The watch window's meta line reads DESIGN-WATCH-WINDOW's `closerWatch` "Checking every 15 minutes until 15:40" for the hour. When the watch is active but paused, the line is "I'll start when the pause ends, then check every 15 minutes for an hour." (`ACTIVITY.closer.paused`). When `watch.everyMinutes` is already 15 the chip is not offered, `close_watch` leaves `allowed.kinds`, and a typed form answers `ACTIVITY.closer.already` "I already check every 15 minutes." Refusals, as app lines with one chip: watch off, "The menu bar watch is off. Turn it on in Settings and I can watch closer." with "Open Settings"; unlicensed, "Watching from the menu bar is in the full version." (`ACTIVITY.refuse.full_version_closer`) with no chip (the lock card rule of DESIGN-CHAT holds: the full version is pitched at most once per check-up, so this line carries no button). Nothing runs on the Mac; the command edits the baseline's `close_watch_until` (never shortening one already set) and wakes the loop (`reconfigure`, `notify_one`, as `close_watch` does today).

### 2f. "Ask me again tomorrow" and "Remind me on Sunday"

Chips on a card the person just declined ("Do this later" result), and the intents `remind_rule` with `when: "tomorrow" | "sunday"`; the typed forms "ask me tomorrow", "remind me on Sunday", "bring this up on Sunday" are caught by the reminder grammar (section 4) before any call:

> **Ask me again tomorrow**
> ✓ OK. I'll bring this up tomorrow when you open me: The firewall is off.

`ACTIVITY.remind.tomorrow(headline)` / `.sunday(headline)`: "OK. I'll bring this up on Sunday when you open me: {headline}." The headline is `guidedFor(ruleId).headline` whole (the headlines are sentences, so nothing is lowered or clipped). "Sunday" is the next Sunday after today; on a Sunday, seven days. The card collapses to the line; the step leaves the plan as a Later does (`snoozed[id]` written). The reminder replaces the 7-day snooze the "Do this later" just wrote; that Later's `laterCount` stands and the reminder adds none, so DESIGN-ACCEPT's third-Later ask is not advanced by a reminder. How it surfaces, since nothing notifies: on the first launch on or after the date, when the rule still fails, the chat's opening message (or the plan screen with the chat off) carries one line before the plan sentence, `ACTIVITY.remind.due` "You asked me to bring this up today: The firewall is off." and that step is first in `plan.steps` for that check-up (a sort key, not a new bucket); the end screen lists it under "Still waiting" until then; the watch window's "Up next" row reads it once DESIGN-WATCH-WINDOW's `watch_set_next` push exists. A reminder that is due and whose rule now passes is cleared silently at `landed()`. On a Mac left closed past the date the line waits for the next open; the app never speaks when closed.

### 2g. "Write this up"

Chip "Write this up" on the end message and the opening, the intent `write_up`, and the typed forms "write this up", "write it up for my son/daughter/someone", "give me a summary I can send":

> **Write this up**
> Here is a plain summary you can send. It has no file names or settings paths in it.
> ┌ Talos Labs check-up, 17 October 2026
> │ Score 72 out of 100, Fair.
> │ Fixed today: The firewall is on. Your Mac stays quiet.
> │ Still to do: Your disk is not locked. If your Mac is lost or stolen, anyone can read your files.
> │ Left for later: A shared folder has no password.
> │ The menu bar watch: nothing changed since the last check.
> │ ChatGPT wrote this. Not verified: "Your Mac goes out with you and its disk is not locked…"
> └
> [Copy it]
> Save as a file…

The text is built by `buildWriteUp(plan, report, fixed, watch, brief, quoteLabel)` in core, pure; `fixed: readonly RuleId[]` is computed by the caller with `rulePasses` (`apps/desktop/src/scan/machine.ts:261`, desktop-side, so core stays free of desktop imports) and `quoteLabel` is `ACTIVITY.writeUp.quote[provider]`. From: `formatDate` for the date; `report.score` and `GRADE_LABELS[grade]`; per fixed id `guided.done` with `DONE_PREFIX` removed; per undone step `guided.headline` and `guided.why` (never `guided.where`); per later id the headline under "Left for later"; per accepted id under "Left alone by choice" (DESIGN-ACCEPT); `WATCH.calm|look|act` after "The menu bar watch: " when the watch is active; the brief's `summary` in quotes after `quoteLabel` only when a validated brief for this `planKey` exists. Every string passes `containsMachineText` before the card renders; a hit drops that line, so a catalogue string that ever gains a path cannot leak through this card. `containsMachineText` (DESIGN-AI-PRESENCE section 6) and `formatDate` (DESIGN-ACCEPT's `labels.ts` addition) are designed, not built; this build lands them first. "Copy it" writes the text to the clipboard with the `CopyButton` confirmation ("Copied" for 1500 ms). "Save as a file…" calls `writeup_save(text)`: Rust opens macOS's save panel with the default name `Talos Labs check-up 2026-10-17.txt`, writes the text (0644) to the path the person chose, and returns `saved | cancelled`; the webview never sees the path; a write error shows `writeUp.failed` "I couldn't save it there. Copy it instead." The preview is a `pre.chat-writeup` at `--text-guided-meta`, scrolling past 8 lines so the primary stays above the fold at 900x600.

### 2h. "What if I leave the disk alone?"

The what-if grammar (section 4) routes the typed question; the intent `what_if` renders the same; the chip "What if I leave it?" appears after a card derived on this Mac and after "Do this later":

> **What if I leave the disk alone?**
> If your Mac is lost or stolen, anyone can read your files.
> Leaving it keeps 18 off your score.
> ▍ **Your disk is not locked** … [Show me where] · I'll do it later · ▸ Explain this
> [Leave it alone] [What's next?] [Show me the list]

Zero calls. The first line is `guided.why`, the risk, because that is the answer; the second is `ACTIVITY.whatIf.cost(points)` where `points` is the finding's `result.points`. No second, higher figure: DESIGN-ACCEPT decided "a second, higher number is the one a person reads", and this document keeps that. For a critical rule a third authored line: `ACTIVITY.whatIf.critical` "This is the one that would hurt most." The card is the step's card (or the Details card for a left-alone rule) and "Leave it alone" is one tap away, so the honest answer and the accept of DESIGN-ACCEPT sit together. When the model is asked instead (a form the grammar misses), its reply streams above the same app card, which the app draws from `what_if` or from `relatedRuleIds`.

### 2i. The model's order

DESIGN-CHAT's `intents` list is kept in the model's order after validation; the first survivor is the live card and the rest are rows. This document adds one rule: when `investigate`, `close_watch`, `remind_rule`, `write_up` or `what_if` survives alongside a `fix_rule`, `fix_batch` or `show_where`, the Mac-changing intent is still drawn as the live card and the others as rows, whatever the model's order, because a card whose primary changes the Mac is the one the person must see whole (DESIGN-WARMTH 6b, the one primary). Within the non-changing kinds the model's order holds. `plan.steps` never re-orders on the model's word.

### 2j. The chip row, amended

DESIGN-CHAT caps the chip row at four and gives a table by state. This document's chips take the slots in this order and DESIGN-CHAT's fill the rest; a chip not in the row is reachable by typing. `chips.ts` is the one place this table lives.

| state | chips |
|---|---|
| opening, something to fix | Do them all · Fix what you can · What is urgent? · Write this up. With two or more day rows in DESIGN-DAILY's history, "Your week" takes the fourth slot and "Write this up" is by typing; while a critical step's card is live, "What is urgent?" is left out (DESIGN-CHAT-UI's opening) and "Show me the list" moves up |
| after "Do this later" | Ask me again tomorrow · What if I leave it? · What's next? · Show me the list (Remind me on Sunday and Check again by typing) |
| after a watch look or act message | Look into this · Watch closer for an hour · What's next? · Show me the list |
| after a `still_failing` result | Check again · Show me where (when a pane) · What's next? · Show me the list |
| after a card derived on this Mac | Leave it alone · What if I leave it? · What's next? · Show me the list |
| after the end message | Write this up · Show me the list · Check again |

"Look into this" and "Watch closer for an hour" need the watch active; "Check again" and "Look into this" on a check-up item need `gate.has("recheck")`; "Write this up" needs a report. A chip whose condition fails is left out and the next in the order moves up.

## 3. Honest limits, and how the app says them

| the agent cannot | the app says | way out |
|---|---|---|
| run anything the model wrote, or a command at all | never arises: there is no field for one; a typed command meets the composer guard, "I can't read file paths or typed instructions. Say it in words and I'll answer." (`CHAT.guard`, DESIGN-CHAT-UI) | type it in words |
| stop a program by name or pid, delete a file or an account | "I can't stop it by name. I can switch off the start-up entry it uses, if it has one." (`ACTIVITY.refuse.cannot_from_here`); the fishy flow's Stop screen when a plist can be bound | "See what to do" |
| see what a program does, scan files, read the socket table on an unsigned build | "I read settings and start-up lists, never what a program does." / the unreadable row with `SOCKET_TABLE_LABEL` | none; the not-antivirus sentence |
| investigate without the watch | "The menu bar watch is off, so I can't look closer from here." (`refuse.watch_off`) with "Open Settings"; unlicensed: "Looking closer from the menu bar is in the full version." (`refuse.full_version`), no button | Settings |
| re-read a check-up area without the full version | "Checking again is in the full version." (`refuse.full_version_recheck`), no chip | the full version |
| make a second judge call in one round, or any call past the cap | "I've used today's allowance for the watch. I'll keep checking the usual way until tomorrow." (`refuse.cap_reached`); the rows still run | the fixed checks run |
| call a provider inside its own limit window | "Your AI has hit its limit for now. I'll keep checking the usual way." (`refuse.limit_reached`); the rows still run | the fixed checks run |
| call with AI on but no provider connected (`choose_judge` is `None`) | no line; the rows run on the rules and carry no badge, as with AI off | Settings |
| act while the person is away, or remind with a notification | "I'll say it next time you open me. I don't send reminders." (`refuse.no_notify`) | the reminder line on the day |
| choose which areas to re-read beyond the model-choosable probes (`MODEL_PROBE_IDS`: 19 today, 24 with DESIGN-THREATS-V2), or run a Rust-only probe (`RUST_PROBE_IDS`: `probe.persistence_signatures`, `probe.ssh_keys`, and `probe.app_signatures` with DESIGN-THREATS-V2) on the model's word | never shown; the validator drops the id and the row never exists | none |
| run two things at once | "I'm in the middle of something. One moment." (`refuse.busy`) when `PlanStore::is_busy()` or a check-up is in flight (`AppState.in_flight > 0`), the two conditions `run_once` already defers on; a running watch cycle is not busy, the round queues behind it | wait |
| explain a watch item with the `explain` task, or fix a watch item as a rule | DESIGN-WATCH-WINDOW's rule: a watch item is a signal id and a kind; the chat offers the watch card and "Ask why this matters" | the fishy flow |

Every refusal is an app line, authored, under the banned-word test, with at most one chip. The model is told nothing about refusals; it sees `allowed.kinds` and names only what is there.

## 4. Contracts

### Core: the activity timeline (`packages/core/src/activity/`, owned here; drawn by DESIGN-CHAT-UI)

One timeline for the chat and the guided step frame. Core holds the types, the reducer and the row selection; it holds no string. DESIGN-CHAT-UI owns the row DOM (`li.chat-activity-row[data-state][data-activity]`), the icons and the state word (`activityWord(event)` in `apps/desktop/src/chat/activityWord.ts` through `CHAT.word`).

```ts
// packages/core/src/activity/types.ts
export type ActivityKind =
  | "preparing"    // "Getting ready" (ACTIVITY.rows.preparing), at the tap, before fix_preview or fix_preview_batch resolves
  | "fixing"       // one row per phase Rust reports: an admin block (ruleId null, count n) or one item (ruleId set)
  | "rechecking"   // "Checking it worked" (ACTIVITY.rows.checking), the verify-after-fix recheck
  | "watching"     // a show card's watcher (CHAT.activity.watching, DESIGN-CHAT-UI)
  | "watch_check"  // "Check now" from the open_watch card (CHAT.activity.watchCheck, DESIGN-CHAT-UI)
  | "probe"        // label WATCH_PROBE_LABELS[probeId], first letter raised; word same | changed | couldn't check
  | "section";     // label SECTION_PROGRESS_LABELS[key]; word still off | now on
export type ActivityStatus = "running" | "done" | "failed";
export type ProbeOutcomeWord = "same" | "changed" | "unavailable";
export type ActivityOutcome = FixError | RecheckResult | ProbeOutcomeWord | "still_off" | "now_on" | "waiting_for_password" | "stale" | null;
export interface ActivityEvent {
  readonly id: string;                 // one per row; a later event with the same id replaces the row in place
  readonly cardId: string;             // the chat card id, the guided step id, or the investigation's roundId
  readonly kind: ActivityKind;
  readonly status: ActivityStatus;
  readonly at: number;                 // Date.now() of the first event for this id
  readonly endedAt: number | null;
  readonly ruleId: RuleId | null;      // fixing: the item; null for an admin block
  readonly count: number | null;       // fixing: items in the admin block; rechecking: areas; watching: polls so far
  readonly step: number | null;        // fixing, user-level: the Running step ("{i} of {n}" with `of`)
  readonly of: number | null;
  readonly probeId: ProbeId | null;    // probe rows
  readonly sectionKey: SectionKey | null;  // section rows
  readonly outcome: ActivityOutcome;   // the state word's input, read by DESIGN-CHAT-UI's activityWord
  readonly modelLine: string | null;   // probe rows only: the judge's probes[].reason after safe_sentence, drawn inside the investigation message
}
// packages/core/src/activity/reduce.ts
export type ActivityState = { readonly events: readonly ActivityEvent[] };   // ordered by `at`, one entry per id
export function reduceActivity(state: ActivityState, event: ActivityEvent): ActivityState;   // upsert by id; never re-orders; `clear` is a fresh state
export function rowsFor(state: ActivityState, cardId: string): readonly ActivityEvent[];        // the card's rows, oldest first
// packages/core/src/activity/label.ts
export function activityLabel(event: ActivityEvent, strings: ActivityStrings): string;   // strings passed in from the desktop copy, so core stays string-free
export interface ActivityStrings { preparing: string; batchAdmin: string; checking: string; watching: string; watchCheck: string; verbs: Readonly<Record<string, string>>; probes: Readonly<Record<ProbeId, string>>; sections: Readonly<Record<SectionKey, string>>; }
```

An investigation round is a message with `cardId = roundId`; its `probe` rows arrive as `ActivityEvent`s from the `watch:activity` event (one `running`, one terminal, same id) and draw under the round's message through `rowsFor` as fix rows draw under a card. `activityLabel` reads `verbs[guided.verb]` for a `fixing` row with a rule (the participle: "Turn it on" becomes "Turning it on"), `batchAdmin` with `count` substituted for an admin block, and the probe and section tables for the two new kinds; a test asserts every catalogue `guided.verb` has a `verbs` entry. At most one `running` row per `cardId` at a time; the reducer does not enforce it, the feeders do, and the harness asserts it (DESIGN-CHAT-UI section 7). No row prints a raw field: the label tables are `WATCH_PROBE_LABELS`, `SECTION_PROGRESS_LABELS`, `ACTIVITY.rows`, `ACTIVITY.verbs` and CHAT-UI's `CHAT.activity.*`. `cardKindFor` (DESIGN-CHAT-UI's `transcript.ts`) maps `investigate`, `close_watch` and `remind_rule` to `null` (app messages), `write_up` to the new `ChatCard.kind` `write_up`, and `what_if` to the rule's own step card kind.

- **Everything else: zero calls.** The plan walk, the checklist, the recheck rows, the closer watch, the reminders, the write-up (the brief is read from `last-brief.json`, never requested for it), the what-if answer.
- **Caching.** The rows are in the transcript for one check-up, cleared with it; a `watch:activity` payload that arrives for a round the chat no longer shows is dropped. The investigation's probe outcomes are merged into the same cycle's `compare`, so a following tick spends nothing re-reading them. Probe minimum intervals (`Probe.min_interval`, M5 or H1, `probes.rs:47`) hold for the person's round too: a probe read under five minutes ago is listed ✓ with its word from `probe_last_outcome` and the Developer line "read 3 minutes ago", not re-run.
- **Time to first row.** The fix rows appear at the tap (the preparing row is dispatched before `fix_preview` resolves). The investigation's first row appears when Rust emits `running` for the first probe, within the sweep's 20 s budget (`SWEEP_BUDGET`, `probes.rs:25`); the lead line is on screen at the tap, so nothing waits in silence. On Claude the judge call adds 3 to 5 s before the model-chosen rows; the code-chosen rows need no call.

## 7. Out of scope

A tool loop of any kind; the model choosing when to investigate, which actions to run, or the plan's order; a second judge call inside one round; a probe outside `MODEL_PROBE_IDS` on the model's word; reminders by notification, email or calendar; a persisted timeline; model text in the write-up beyond the labelled brief; a write-up as PDF or HTML; sharing from the app (Mail, Messages); a closer watch beyond 60 and 1440 minutes; "look into this" without the watch's rules (no watch, no rules, no round); any change to the fix engine's tables, the batch rules, the playbook, the rating, the hysteresis or the notification policy; Windows and Linux; localisation beyond British English.

## Open questions for the owner

1. **Saving the write-up to a file.** It adds `rfd`, a Rust-only dependency, one command that takes authored text, and a row in SECURITY-MODEL. The clipboard alone needs none of that. Default here: build both, the clipboard as the primary and the file as the link.
2. **One counted judge call for "Look into this".** The alternative is zero calls (the rules choose the probes every time), which loses the grey reason lines and the model's choice of probes. Default here: one call when AI is on and the cap allows, the rules otherwise.
3. **The closer watch at one hour.** DESIGN-WATCH's recover screen keeps 24 h at 15 minutes; this document offers an hour from the chat. A third length (a day, from the chat) is one more chip. Default here: an hour from the chat, a day from the recover screen, no third.
4. **Activity rows on the guided step frame.** They replace the one status line with a list of up to four rows under the headline; DESIGN-V03 allows "at most two sentences" and this reads as a list, like `GuidedList`. The alternative keeps the one line in the walk-through and shows rows in the chat only. Default here: rows in both.
5. **Reminders steering the plan order.** A due reminder puts its step first for that check-up; the alternative leaves the order to points and only says the line. Default here: first.
